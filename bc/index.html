Printing at The Game Crafter, 100 deck ideas, and the box

A print plan around TGC's rule for horizontal cards (backs turned 180 degrees) with a flip-test card, a hundred ways to organise decks around 21-card sheets with their box sizes, and a tuck box for the standard two-sheet deck.

How to print the cards at The Game Crafter given its rule for horizontal cards, a hundred ways to organise the decks around its 21-card sheets, and a box for the standard deck.

The print plan

The Game Crafter's rule. Every card image is uploaded in portrait. For a card used horizontally to read the same way up on both sides, the front or the back image has to be turned a further 180°, and they suggest turning the back. People who skipped this report backs arriving upside down; designers who avoid the issue use backs that look the same either way up, which ours don't (each back carries text).

How we meet it. The exporter turns each landscape face a quarter turn anticlockwise into portrait, and each back a quarter turn clockwise: the back is the face's turn plus 180°, exactly TGC's instruction. Turned over left to right, a card's back then reads the right way up, with the stripe on the same physical edge as the front's.

  1. Choose the size: 63 words a side (8.6 pt, 6,257 cards for the Bible) or 72 (8.4 pt, 5,563). Everything below works for either.
  2. Export a deck with python3 tgc.py export BOOK: one 675 × 1125 px PNG per face and per back, named Book 001[face,1].png and [back,1], drawn at 1200 dpi and reduced, sRGB. The first card is the flip test (000a), then the title card (000), then the numbered cards. An odd last side gets the branded back.
  3. Upload: in TGC, create a game, add a Business Deck, and drop the PNGs in. The names pair each face with its back. Every card has its own back, so leave the deck's shared back empty.
  4. Proof online: in TGC's proofing step, check that each face and back has the stripe edge at the top of the portrait image, and nothing important is outside the safe zone.
  5. Order one copy of one two-sheet deck with its box (normal queue, about 1–3 weeks). Hosea is two sheets exactly.
  6. Check the proof deck: turn the flip-test card over left to right (the back reads upright, stripe on the left), then check drift on the stripe, the book colour against the screen, 8.6 pt legibility, and that the cards sort by their numbers (TGC does not sort a deck's cards before packing).
  7. Fix and reorder if anything is off; then print in volume. TGC's bulk price falls to about $1.08 a sheet from 100 copies.

What I think

  1. Make the two-sheet deck the standard: 42 cards in the Bridge Tuck Box (54), the smallest box that carries it. One box, one price (about $3.78 of cards), and twelve books or pairings fill it exactly: Hosea; Habakkuk and Lamentations; Joel and Micah; Ruth and 1 John; Galatians or Ephesians with 2 Timothy, and more.
  2. Use one-sheet decks as gifts and samplers: 1 Peter, or Malachi and Jude, in a Poker 36 tuck for $1.89 of cards.
  3. Fill spare places on purpose rather than leave them blank: a contents card and the wallet promo card cost nothing extra when the sheet is already paid for.
  4. Let big books be multi-sheet decks in the ladder's larger boxes, and gather a canon section's tucks in a slipcase (the Small Stout box holds about 310).
  5. Colour the spine by book group so a shelf of decks reads like the canon.

The box

Designed for the Bridge Tuck Box (54), the smallest box that carries a 42-card deck, in LB's style. The front has the title card's Chi Rho and the book's name, the stripe on the right edge, and the playing-card footer. The stripe continues round the corner as a coloured spine with the name reversed out. The back and lid carry the branded tessellation in the book's colour, and there is a thumb notch on the front. TGC's box template can't be downloaded from this sandbox, so the dieline is drawn to the box's nominal interior (2.3 × 3.56 × 0.72 in); the art is laid out panel by panel to move onto TGC's template once it is downloaded from the product page.

Resolution and file format

The Game Crafter prints at 300 dpi and gives every template in exact pixels: 675 × 1125 for a Business Deck card. Any other size is resampled by their uploader, which they warn can change how it looks, so a larger upload buys nothing. The export exceeds the spec where it counts instead. Each side is drawn at 1200 dpi from the vector design and reduced to exactly 675 × 1125 px with Lanczos resampling, which gives cleaner thin strokes and small type than a direct 300 dpi render. It is saved as a flattened RGB PNG with an embedded sRGB profile and 300 dpi recorded. Alongside the upload set, 600 dpi PNG masters and the vector SVGs are kept for any other printer.

Lamination options at credit-card size

  1. Plastic card construction. Plastic-card printers print a PVC core and fuse a clear overlay laminate over it, gloss or matte. The card is waterproof all through, with no clear border. This is the closest to a real credit card.
  2. Overlay patch from a card printer's lamination module: a 0.5 or 1 mil clear film (or a holographic one) bonded over each printed side.
  3. Sealed pouch, the relic-card finish. The card ends up exactly credit-card size with a clear 1/8 in border all round. With two 10 mil films over a 12 pt card, it is about 32 mil thick, close to a real credit card's 30 mil. The printed area shrinks to 1-7/8 × 3-1/8 in, which brings the text down to 8.15 pt with the glyph alone in the footer, or 8.55 pt with no footer.
  4. Not waterproof: film lamination or soft-touch on paper leaves the cut edges open.

Deck thickness

A standard credit card is 30 mil (0.76 mm), so 55 of them stack about 42 mm, more than twice a playing-card deck. Pouch-laminated cards stack the same or more. For a deck, ask plastic-card printers for 20 mil (about 28 mm for 55) or 13–15 mil graphic-quality PVC (about 18–21 mm, close to playing cards). Single wallet cards can stay 30 mil. No off-the-shelf tuck box fits CR80, so a credit-card deck needs a custom tuck or rigid box from a packaging house.

The design at credit-card size

Recommendation

For the product, get quotes from two or three plastic-card printers for a 55-card set with a different image on every card and both sides printed. Ask for 20 mil or thinner PVC, a gloss or matte overlay, rounded corners, and 5–10 sets. That gives the real credit-card feel and waterproofing with no hand labour. For proofs, and for a relic-style keepsake edition, print paper cards at 1-7/8 × 3-1/8 in and seal them in 10 mil credit-card pouches. A card printer of our own makes sense only if we will print in small batches often; at about $1 a card it is the most expensive per deck.
