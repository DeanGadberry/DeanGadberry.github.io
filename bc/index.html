The text block is now 2 in × 85 mm, which fits every regional business-card size, so a deck paginates once and prints anywhere. All eleven design options can be flipped through: turn the card over, change the sample book, and compare US and European sizes.

Business-card printing is the most widely available option, but the standard size changes by region. The text block on these cards is now 2 in × 85 mm, which fits inside the US card (3.5 × 2 in), the European card (85 × 55 mm), the Australian (90 × 55), Japanese (91 × 55) and Chinese (90 × 54) cards, and a credit card (85.6 × 54). A deck paginates once and keeps the same card numbers wherever it is printed. Only the side margins and the depth of the band change.

Iteration 004 · 2 October 2026

Credit-card size: unique cards, both sides, and lamination

Whether a credit-card-size (CR80) deck with a different card on every card, printed on both sides, can be made, and how to waterproof it. Also how large the text can be: 9.25 pt on a CR80 card, 8.15 to 8.55 pt on a paper card sealed in a credit-card pouch.

Yes: a deck of credit-card-size cards, each card unique and printed on both sides, is feasible. It just doesn't come from the game-card printers we've used. MakePlayingCards and The Game Crafter offer no credit-card size. The workable routes are plastic-card printers, a card printer of our own, and paper cards sealed in credit-card pouches.

9.25 pt

is the largest text that holds three verses a side on a full credit card (CR80, 2.125 × 3.375 in). That is a little more than the 2 × 3.5 in card allows, because the extra width saves more lines than the lost height costs. With the glyph alone in the footer: 3.03 verses a side, 5,139 cards. With no footer: 3.25 verses a side, 4,779 cards. Esther 8:9 again sets the ceiling.

Lamination options at credit-card size

  1. Plastic card construction. Plastic-card printers print a PVC core and fuse a clear overlay laminate over it, gloss or matte. The card is waterproof all through, with no clear border. This is the closest to a real credit card.
  2. Overlay patch from a card printer's lamination module: a 0.5 or 1 mil clear film (or a holographic one) bonded over each printed side.
  3. Sealed pouch, the relic-card finish. The card ends up exactly credit-card size with a clear 1/8 in border all round. With two 10 mil films over a 12 pt card, it is about 32 mil thick, close to a real credit card's 30 mil. The printed area shrinks to 1-7/8 × 3-1/8 in, which brings the text down to 8.15 pt with the glyph alone in the footer, or 8.55 pt with no footer.
  4. Not waterproof: film lamination or soft-touch on paper leaves the cut edges open.

Deck thickness

A standard credit card is 30 mil (0.76 mm), so 55 of them stack about 42 mm, more than twice a playing-card deck. Pouch-laminated cards stack the same or more. For a deck, ask plastic-card printers for 20 mil (about 28 mm for 55) or 13–15 mil graphic-quality PVC (about 18–21 mm, close to playing cards). Single wallet cards can stay 30 mil. No off-the-shelf tuck box fits CR80, so a credit-card deck needs a custom tuck or rigid box from a packaging house.

The design at credit-card size

Recommendation

For the product, get quotes from two or three plastic-card printers for a 55-card set with a different image on every card and both sides printed. Ask for 20 mil or thinner PVC, a gloss or matte overlay, rounded corners, and 5–10 sets. That gives the real credit-card feel and waterproofing with no hand labour. For proofs, and for a relic-style keepsake edition, print paper cards at 1-7/8 × 3-1/8 in and seal them in 10 mil credit-card pouches. A card printer of our own makes sense only if we will print in small batches often; at about $1 a card it is the most expensive per deck.

Iteration 002 · 2 October 2026

The playing-card design at business size, and how large the text can be

Card generation reworked to the playing cards' own design and pagination, scaled to business size. With the whole KJV paginated at every size, three verses a side holds up to 9 pt on a portrait card with the top band, slightly larger than the playing cards' 8.8 pt.

Card generation is reworked to the playing cards' own design, now drawn in points and scaled from the body size, so the type can grow without re-tuning every offset. The cards use the playing cards' pagination too: whole verses, two or more a side, the fewest sides per book, and then the most even split.

9 pt

is the largest text that still averages three verses a side, on a portrait card (2 × 3.5 in) with the band on top. That is slightly larger than the playing cards' 8.8 pt. Across the whole KJV it gives 3.09 verses a side. Sides are filled to 83% ± 10%, and the Bible comes to 5,039 cards.

Just above 9 pt, Esther 8:9, the longest verse in the KJV, no longer fits on one side, so whole-verse pagination breaks. Getting 9 pt took one change: the side margins go from 11 to 10 pt, still outside the 1/8 in safe line. At 9 pt, 130 of 10,077 sides (1.3%) hold a single verse because it is too long to share. Every other side keeps the playing cards' two-verse minimum.

Three verses per physical card, meaning 1.5 a side, isn't possible. The longest verses stop fitting long before the text is large enough for that.

Recommended: portrait, top band, 9 pt

Business-card guidance puts body text at 8 pt or more, treats 7 pt as a fine-print floor, and suggests 9–10 pt for older readers. EB Garamond's x-height is small (0.40 em), so 8.5 pt reads like about 7.5 pt of a typical sans. That is why 8.5 pt is the smallest size I'd ship, and why the 10 pt large-print edition (J) is a real option rather than a novelty.

At 8.5 pt, 38 of the 66 books fit a single 55-card deck in option A, and 42 in option D. The rest need the larger volume decks, as with the playing cards.

Landscape or portrait? Landscape gives about 60-character lines, comfortable for the Gospels, the Epistles and narrative. Portrait gives about 32-character lines, which suit Psalms and Proverbs. Portrait (E) measured about 10% more efficient, partly because the band is a smaller share of a taller card and partly because of slightly narrower margins. Rounded corners are the better choice for a card that lives in a wallet: square corners dog-ear.

Wallet fit: a 3.5 × 2 in card is 1/8 in longer than a credit card. It fits a bill compartment and many card slots, but not all. The CR80 size (H) fits every slot.

More ideas

  1. A gilt edge for free. The band bleeds off the top, so a stacked deck shows a stripe of the book colour along its top edge.
  2. Progress mark. A small tick in the band steps from left to right through the book. Fan the deck and it draws a diagonal, so you can see where you are.
  3. Memory deck. Option M as its own product, such as 100 key verses, or as the bonus card in every deck.
  4. Relic keepsake. Option I sealed in a pouch, as a gift or promo single.
  5. Large-print edition at 10 pt (J).
  6. Pocket Testament edition with run-in verses (D): 28% fewer cards and fewer decks per book.
  7. Foil. MPC prints gold and silver: a foil Chi Rho in the band on title cards.
  8. Chapter openers. A large chapter numeral set into the band where a chapter begins.
  9. Turn cue. A small ▸ at the foot of the front when the passage continues on the back.
  10. Box motto. ΤΑΥΤΑ ΜΕΛΕΤΑ carried over to the business-size box.
  11. Wallet singles. CR80 plastic cards (H) of single passages, from a plastic-card maker, as promotional cards.
