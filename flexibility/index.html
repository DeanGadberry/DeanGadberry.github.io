How it works

Two kinds of sessions, and that’s the whole system:

  • Daily — 10–15 minutes, easy, almost every day. It keeps what you’ve gained.[1]
  • Focus — 20–30 minutes, hard, 2–3 times a week, right after lifting while you’re warm. This is what builds new range.[1]

Rules. Warm up first. Use contract-relax: push into the stretch 5–10 seconds, let go, breathe out, sink deeper. Strong stretch is fine; sharp or pinching pain means back off. Keep the back flat in the pancake — fold from the hips. Expect many months for a pancake and longer for the backbend.[1]

What the research says

  • Weekly time matters most. A review of 23 studies found at least about 5 minutes of stretching per muscle per week was needed for gains, and more stretching days per week helped more than longer single sessions.[2]
  • Static stretching works best for long-term range, though every method improved it.[2]
  • Lifting through a full range builds flexibility too — a 2021 meta-analysis found strength training and stretching improved range of motion about equally.[3] Loaded stretching gets you both.
  • Stretching before lifting is fine if each hold is under about 60 seconds. Longer holds can sap strength for that session, so save long holds for after.[4]
  • Hard sessions twice a week, easy practice most days is the structure flexibility coaches use; once you reach a goal, roughly a sixth of the work it took to build it maintains it.[1]

Backbend ladder

Toes-to-head is the top rung. Hold each until it’s comfortable for 3 sets of 30 seconds, then move up.

  1. Sphinx, then cobra
  2. Bridge from the floor, bent arms is fine
  3. Straight-arm bridge, shoulders pushed over the hands
  4. Bridge, walking the hands closer to the feet
  5. Wall walk-downs into a bridge
  6. Kneeling backbend (camel) → hands to heels
  7. King pigeon — foot to head, one leg
  8. Chest stand, then toes toward the head

Most backbend limits are tight hip flexors and shoulders, not the spine. Couch stretch and overhead shoulder work count as backbend training. Build slowly: the lower back takes months to adapt, and any sharp or nerve-like pain means stop.

Loaded stretching for lifters

  • Jefferson curl — slow, rounded-spine toe touch holding a light weight. Start with 10–20 lb.
  • Seated pancake good morning — sit in the straddle with a plate or bar, fold forward with a flat back.
  • Deep-stretch RDLs — pause 2 seconds at the bottom.
  • Weighted pancake hold — plate on the upper back, 30–60 seconds.
  • Cossack squats and ATG split squats — strength at the end of your hip range.

2–3 sets each, after lifting. Go light and progress slowly — strength at end range is what makes new range stick.

Methods & schools of thought

Stretch Therapy

Who
Kit Laughlin — Stretch Therapy
Idea
Contract-relax done gently and precisely, often with a partner, plus relaxation to calm the nervous system’s protective tension. The source of many adult pancakes.
  • One-Hour Beginner Stretch Therapy ClassKit Laughlin · start here

Loaded progressive stretching

Who
Emmet Louis (Mobility Development), Tom Merrick (Bodyweight Warrior)
Idea
Treat flexibility like strength: sets, reps, load and progression in the stretched position. Twice-weekly hard sessions plus easy daily practice — the structure this plan uses.[1]
  • Interview with Emmet Louis: Flexibility for LiftersPropane · long interview, worth a listen

Functional Range Conditioning (FRC)

Who
Dr. Andreo Spina
Idea
CARs — slow, controlled circles of each joint at its outer limits, done daily. PAILs/RAILs — hard isometric contractions at end range to strengthen and claim new range.[5]
Try
Add hip and shoulder CARs (5 slow circles each way) to your daily session.

Knees Over Toes

Who
Ben Patrick
Idea
Strengthen joints through their full range from the ground up: backward walking, tibialis raises, ATG split squats, Jefferson curls. Popular for knee health.[6]
  • How To Build a Bulletproof Body: Ben PatrickFitnessFAQs Podcast · interview

Gymnastics & calisthenics mobility

Who
Antranik, GMB Fitness, FitnessFAQs
Idea
Mobility in service of skills — bridges, handstands, L-sits. Clear progressions and strong shoulder work.

Classic martial-arts stretching

Who
Thomas Kurz (Stretching Scientifically), Pavel Tsatsouline (Relax into Stretch)
Idea
Dynamic stretching in the morning, isometric stretching for strength at length, relaxed static stretching to finish. Built for splits.

Checkpoints — every 4 weeks

  • Pancake: measure chest-to-floor distance in a wide straddle. Take a side photo.
  • Toe touch: standing, knees straight — fingertips, knuckles, then palms flat.
  • Bridge: measure hands-to-heels distance.
